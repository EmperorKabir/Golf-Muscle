## Product specification
### Core concept (user, 2026-08-04)
- Android app: interactive 3D model of a human body performing a golf swing.
- Gestures: pinch to zoom; **one finger spins the model** (orbit). Confirmed 2026-08-04.
- Time slider at the bottom spanning the swing from address to follow-through. User can play the swing through, scrub to any point, and watch in slow motion — understanding muscle use over time, not just at static points.
- At any point in time, muscles are colour-coded with a gradient: red = most intense activation, blending down to transparent = zero use at that instant. Intensity corresponds to that muscle's activation/exertion at that moment.
- The model must look good from all angles.
- Muscle grouping granularity: not so few that relevantly different muscles are merged; not so many that it reads like a biology textbook or splits groups below swing-relevance. When unsure, err toward MORE groups — unused muscles are useful contrast.
- Personalisation dimensions to design for (later): limb length, height, sex, shot type, club.
- V1 subject: 189 cm male, long femurs, 8-iron, right-handed.

### Decisions locked 2026-08-04 (user answers)
- **D-001 Accuracy is the project's highest priority.** Muscle-activation data must be researched from as many sources as possible — everywhere available — then reasoned into an applied model. Requires cross-referencing many independent sources for a full, well-rounded understanding of which muscles fire, at what intensity, at what time in the swing, and how activation ramps up/backs off across swing, golfer and club. Context efficiency is expected in gathering this.
- **D-002 Visual style: transparent artist's-mannequin wireframe.** Like a wooden posable artist's figure, except the wood itself is transparent and only the *borders* of each muscle zone are drawn as single-wire outlines. Colour fill appears only where a muscle is active. Zone borders are anatomically correct — real muscle boundaries, not simplified geometric shapes. NOT a full medical model: skin, sinew and tendons are irrelevant and excluded. Aesthetic serves comprehension of the swing, nothing else.
- **D-003 The figure animates through the full swing.** Body moves — arms, hips, spine — while colours change. Rationale (user): the user must be able to internalise which muscles fire at a given pose they can physically replicate. Applies to all swing types/clubs/golfers. Accepted as a large build.
- **D-004 The 3D model is generated in-project, not sourced.** Accepted as a large build. Gate before committing to it: produce at least 10 static-view renders viewable in a browser tab, each with bullet-pointed features and differences, for user selection.
- **D-005 Modern devices only.** Phones and tablets; no support obligation for old/low-end hardware. Visual quality takes precedence.
- **D-006 Free app.** No paid tiers.
- **D-007 Right-handed for V1.** Left-handed is a whole-system mirror and treated as straightforward once the right-handed path exists — not a separate build.

### Research findings that constrain the spec (added as they land — each needs a user decision or a labelled assumption)
- **F-001 Activation exceeds 100% MVC in the golf swing.** Erector spinae means reach 83–106% MVC in acceleration (Marta et al. 2013; J Sports Sci 2018). Isometric MVC underestimates ballistic activation across sports (giant slalom 283%, baseball pitching 226% — Clarys et al. 2010). **Consequence:** the colour scale must not hard-clip at 100%. Needs an explicit decision on ceiling and saturation behaviour. Source: `docs/research/06-activation-curve-and-colour-method.md`.
- **F-002 Club barely changes trunk activation.** Direct EMG comparison across driver/4-iron/7-iron/pitching wedge found **no significant trunk activation difference** (p>0.05). Lower-limb activation does vary by club, but non-monotonically (4-iron > pitching wedge; driver never shown to exceed irons). **Consequence:** club personalisation can confidently modulate kinematics and timing, but activation magnitude only as a labelled inference. Source: `docs/research/05-club-shot-golfer-and-anthropometry.md`.
- **F-003 No golf-specific evidence exists for femur length effects.** Zero peer-reviewed sources link femur length or leg-to-torso ratio to golf swing mechanics or muscle demand. Only cross-domain, non-peer-reviewed strength-training inference is available. **Consequence:** the V1 subject's defining "long femurs" trait cannot be evidence-based. It must be modelled from first principles and labelled in-product as modelled, not measured. Source: `docs/research/05-club-shot-golfer-and-anthropometry.md`.
- **F-004 No iron-specific swing timing data exists.** All published phase durations are driver-based (backswing ≈730–850 ms, downswing ≈230–300 ms, ratio ≈3:1). Any 8-iron timing must be scaled from driver data and labelled an unverified assumption. Source: `docs/research/01-phase-taxonomy-and-timing.md`.
- **F-005 A pure red-to-transparent ramp fails accessibility and mid-range discrimination.** Red-green colour deficiency affects up to 8% of people; flat-luminance red gradients collapse to indistinguishable shades (Crameri et al., Nature Communications 11:5444, 2020). **Consequence:** retain the red-hot metaphor but pair it with a monotonic luminance ramp. Awaiting user decision via study 10 of the model style studies. Source: `docs/research/06-activation-curve-and-colour-method.md`.
- **F-006 Partial and full swings share one sequencing strategy.** Proximal-to-distal sequencing held across partial wedge shots and full swings, both sexes, both skill tiers (n=45, Sports Biomechanics 2010). **Consequence:** shot-length personalisation scales amplitude and timing only — the sequence pattern is not re-modelled per shot. Source: `docs/research/05-club-shot-golfer-and-anthropometry.md`.
- **F-008 "Acceleration phase" means opposite things in two literatures.** Peer-reviewed golf EMG (Bechler, Watkins, Pink/Jobe/Perry, Marta) defines acceleration as **impact → early follow-through** (i.e. *after* impact). Coaching sources including TPI use it for the final part of the downswing *before* impact. Any phase-averaged figure labelled "acceleration" is ambiguous until its source is checked. **Consequence:** adopt the peer-reviewed convention internally; user-facing labels must avoid the word entirely and say "just before impact" / "just after impact". Mixing the two would silently corrupt the timeline. Source: `docs/research/03-lower-limb-and-hip-activation.md`.
- **F-009 "Never measured" must not render as "not working".** Five lower-limb muscles have zero golf EMG data anywhere: gluteus minimus, the deep hip external rotators, tensor fasciae latae, soleus in isolation, and iliopsoas — the last gap explicitly confirmed in print by Marta et al. 2012. Under the current colour system an unmeasured muscle would be drawn transparent, which asserts to the user that it does no work in the golf swing. That is a false claim and directly undermines D-001. **Consequence:** the renderer needs a third visual state distinct from both "active" and "inactive" — the muscle zone outline present but marked as unmeasured. Affects T-012, T-014 and T-021. Raised from the research, not from the user's brief.
- **F-010 Published %MVC figures use three incompatible units.** %MMT (manual muscle test — Pink/Jobe/Perry/Watkins), %MVC (isometric max — Sorbie, Li, Cole & Grimshaw) and %EMGmax (peak within the swing — Marta). A muscle at "60%" in one study is not comparable to "60%" in another. **Consequence:** the activation model cannot pool raw published numbers; T-012 must define a conversion or segregation strategy before any value reaches the renderer. This is the single largest obstacle to a unified model. Source: `docs/research/02-trunk-and-core-activation.md`.
- **F-011 Erector spinae magnitude is genuinely disputed, by 2–3×.** Li et al. 2023 report 13–52% MVC across phases; Sorbie et al. 2018 report 67–106% MVC for the same muscle group. Not reconcilable from the published methods (different electrode sites, skill mixes, MVC references). **Consequence:** carry both ranges and show uncertainty rather than picking one. Affects the single most prominent muscle group in the back view. Source: `docs/research/02-trunk-and-core-activation.md`.
- **F-012 Highest verified activation anywhere in the swing: trail gluteus maximus, 62–72% EMGmax** (Marta et al. 2013), narrowly ahead of trail external oblique at 59–67%. Both peak forward swing → acceleration. **Consequence:** these two set the top of the colour scale for muscles with trustworthy numbers — and both are posterior, invisible in the current front-only style studies.
- **F-013 Three more muscles have zero golf EMG data**, confirmed by three independent research passes: transversus abdominis, multifidus, quadratus lumborum. Internal oblique is a near-gap (measured but amplitude never reported). Latissimus dorsi has **no numeric value at any phase** in any accessible source — qualitative timing only, despite being described as the primary power contributor of the early downswing. Extends F-009's unmeasured-state requirement to the trunk.
- **F-014 The literature never measures address, top of backswing, or impact.** All five-phase studies treat these as boundary *instants*, not sampled bins. **Consequence:** any value the app displays at address or impact — including the moment users will most want to inspect — is interpolation, not measurement, and must be presented as such. Source: `docs/research/02-trunk-and-core-activation.md`.
- **F-007 Draw/fade is distal, not trunk.** Swing path and lead-forearm supination differ significantly between shapes; pelvis and thorax rotation do not. **Consequence:** shot-shaping affects forearm activation, not core. Source: `docs/research/05-club-shot-golfer-and-anthropometry.md`.
- **F-015 (LARGELY SUPERSEDED 2026-08-04 by F-027 — Pink 1990's full numeric grid was subsequently recovered; retained for the Kao 1995 portion, which remains inaccessible.) The classic shoulder/scapular EMG papers' numeric tables are inaccessible, not merely unsummarised.** Jobe 1986 (rotator cuff), Jobe 1989 (men/women), Pink 1990 (shoulder), Kao 1995 (scapular) are paywalled on SAGE/AJSM; every legitimate mirror (PubMed, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar) surfaces only qualitative abstract text ("subscapularis active during acceleration"), never the in-paper %MVC tables. Extends F-013 beyond latissimus dorsi to nearly the entire shoulder girdle: pectoralis major, all three deltoid heads, all four rotator-cuff muscles individually, trapezius by region, rhomboids, levator scapulae and serratus anterior have **no phase-by-phase numeric value from a primary source** — only pooled peak figures via a secondary review (Escamilla & Andrews 2009: rotator cuff 28–68% MVIC, serratus anterior ~70%, trapezius 42–52%, rhomboids/levator scapulae ~60%, none broken out by phase or side). **Consequence:** T-012/T-021 cannot build a phase-resolved shoulder-girdle activation curve from primary numbers alone; either accept the qualitative-pattern + single-peak-value model as the working data, or budget a follow-up pass through a library/institutional-access route. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md`.
- **F-016 A fourth incompatible unit joins F-010's list: raw, unnormalised EMG amplitude (µV).** Two recent forearm studies (Bochnia et al. 2024; Grieß et al. 2026) report only raw microvolt amplitudes, not %MVC/%MMT/%EMGmax — a fourth convention on top of F-010's three, this time from upper-limb sources. Their numbers are usable only as within-study relative shape (phase-to-phase, lead-vs-trail, condition-vs-condition), never as absolute magnitude, and must never be plotted on the same colour scale as any %MVC-family figure. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md`.
- **F-017 Highest verified true-%MVC figure found anywhere in the upper limb: wrist flexors, 90.77% MVC burst at contact/impact** (Glazebrook et al. 1994); wrist extensors ramp 33.59% MVC (address) → 58.77% MVC (contact) in the same true-%MVC study. **Consequence:** these are the most trustworthy candidates (real %MVC, not %MMT or raw µV) for anchoring the upper-limb end of the colour scale alongside F-012's trunk/hip figures — cross-reference before finalising T-021's scale.
- **F-018 (CORRECTED 2026-08-04 — supersedes the original wording) Four upper-limb muscle groups have zero golf EMG data anywhere**, confirmed by targeted search each: teres major, teres minor, supinator, and all intrinsic hand/grip muscles (first dorsal interosseous, thenar, hypothenar). **Triceps brachii and brachioradialis were wrongly included in the original F-018 and are now removed**: Pezarat-Correia et al. 2006 (Proc ECSS Lausanne 3(1):70-85) reports **trail-arm triceps long head 50% MVC peaking in the downswing** and **trail-arm brachioradialis 45% MVC peaking in the backswing** (also biceps 26%, latissimus dorsi 53%) — n=3, trail arm only, 3-phase scheme, conference proceedings, so indicative rather than established. **Lead-arm triceps and brachioradialis do remain unmeasured.** Pectoralis major is never split into sternal vs clavicular heads in any source. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md`.
- **F-019 Lead/trail forearm asymmetry is the best-corroborated upper-limb finding, but its magnitude is not cross-comparable.** Three independent datasets (Robinson 2023 on ECU; Bochnia 2024 raw-µV on ECRB/FCU; Farber 2009 %MMT on pronator teres) agree on the *shape*: trail forearm drives the downswing/release (peaks in forward swing/acceleration/downswing), lead forearm stabilises through backswing and controls/decelerates through follow-through (peaks in takeaway and follow-through). Farber 2009 additionally found this reverses by skill: amateurs over-activate trail-arm pronator teres in the downswing (medial-epicondylitis risk); professionals show relatively more lead-arm activity late in the swing. Because the three studies use three different units (F-010/F-016), only the timing pattern is safe to encode in T-012/T-021, not a shared magnitude curve. Grip force itself is lead-hand-dominant throughout (Langlais & Broker 2014: dominant force from the lead hand's last three fingers) but absolute grip-force magnitude (N/kg) is sensor-method-dependent by a factor of ~3× between glove-mounted and handle-mounted sensors (Schmidt 2007, secondary-sourced) — treat any single absolute grip-force number as illustrative, not a physiological constant. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md`.

- **F-020 Blank cells in the McHardy & Pollard tables mean "not top two", NOT "inactive" and NOT "no data".** Tables 3 and 4 list only the two most-active muscles per side per phase. Lower trapezius, rhomboids in 4 of 5 phases, levator scapulae in 4 of 5 phases and latissimus dorsi in **all** phases have no value there despite being active. **Consequence:** the highest-risk misreading in the whole dataset — reading blanks as zero would draw genuinely working muscles as transparent. Source: `docs/research/07-verbatim-source-tables.md`.
- **F-021 The scapular gap is now filled.** Kim et al. 2004 Table 2 (PMID 15262661, citation independently verified) gives a complete grid for levator scapulae, rhomboids, trapezius and serratus anterior, both sides, all five phases. Qualitative ordinal (+ ≈ 15–20% MMT), and a secondary reproduction of Kao et al. 1995 rather than new measurement. Kim's phase scheme splits takeaway from backswing and does not split follow-through — **do not merge cell-to-cell with McHardy's scheme**. Lead-side serratus anterior shows constant low activity across the entire swing, a distinctive pattern with a real fatigue mechanism attached. Source: `docs/research/07-verbatim-source-tables.md`.
- **F-022 Revision to F-012 — trail gluteus maximus confirmed dominant by two independent routes.** McHardy Table 4 gives trail gluteus maximus at **100% and 98% %MMT** in forward swing (upper and lower portions), against Marta's 62–72% **%EMGmax**. Different units under F-010, not directly comparable — but two independent studies using independent normalisation both identify it as the dominant muscle of the downswing. That convergence is the strongest single signal in the dataset. Left vastus lateralis 88% %MMT and bilateral pectoralis major 93% %MMT (acceleration) are the next tier. Source: `docs/research/07-verbatim-source-tables.md`.
- **F-023 The "acceleration" clash from F-008 exists *inside* the peer-reviewed literature, not just between research and coaching.** McHardy & Pollard explicitly define acceleration as "horizontal club to impact" — i.e. **ending at** impact — while the Bechler/Marta lineage places it **after** impact. **Consequence:** F-008's remedy is upgraded from advisable to mandatory — never rely on a phase label; always read the source's own printed definition before ingesting any value.
- **F-024 Latissimus dorsi numbers now exist, but its peak phase is disputed three ways.** Pink et al. 1990 %MMT: trail 9/50/47/39/28 and lead 17/46/31/32/18 across takeaway → late follow-through (peak = forward swing, both sides; single-source, could not be cross-validated). Peak phase: Pink says forward swing, Kim says downswing+acceleration, Marta says acceleration. Probably a phase-boundary artefact but genuinely different as printed. Partially closes the F-013 latissimus gap. Source: `docs/research/07-verbatim-source-tables.md`.
- **F-025 The left-handed mirror assumption (D-007) is flagged as unverified by the literature itself.** McHardy & Pollard p.803: "it is an assumption that EMG activity in the left handed golfer is a mirror image of the right handed golfer." **Consequence:** D-007 remains the right implementation choice but must be labelled as an assumption in-product, not presented as established equivalence.
- **F-027 The Pink 1990 shoulder grid is recovered — the shoulder girdle now has a full phase-resolved, side-resolved numeric model.** A scanned copy of Pink, Jobe & Perry 1990 yielded all five phases × both sides × 8 muscles in %MMT, cross-validated 9/9 against McHardy & Pollard Table 3. Trail: pectoralis major 12/64/**93**/74/37, subscapularis 16/49/**68**/64/56, supraspinatus **25**/14/12/7/7, infraspinatus **27**/13/7/12/9, anterior deltoid 5/**21**/10/11/8. Lead: pectoralis major 21/18/**93**/74/39, subscapularis **33**/29/41/23/35, supraspinatus 21/21/18/**28**/28, infraspinatus 14/16/27/**61**/40, anterior deltoid 13/9/10/21/**26**. Middle deltoid 2–8% and posterior deltoid 5–24% throughout, both sides. **Consequence:** T-012/T-021 can now build a real shoulder activation curve rather than the qualitative fallback F-015 assumed. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md` §1A.
- **F-028 The dominant organising pattern of the upper limb is side-inverted timing: trail peaks early, lead peaks late.** Supraspinatus, infraspinatus, middle trapezius, anterior deltoid and ECRB all peak on the trail side during takeaway/backswing and on the lead side during follow-through. Lead infraspinatus is the clearest case — 14% at takeaway rising to **61% at early follow-through** (vs trail's 12%), the lead shoulder acting as the swing's decelerator. Exceptions: subscapularis (trail-dominant throughout, 68 vs 41) and pectoralis major (93% both sides at acceleration; asymmetry is in timing — trail 64% vs lead 18% at forward swing — not peak). Corroborated by injury epidemiology: >90% of golfers' shoulder problems involve the lead shoulder, ~3× the trail rate (Kim et al. 2004). **Consequence:** the app's headline visual story for the upper body is this early-trail/late-lead handoff; encode it per muscle per side, never as one global rule. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md` §5/§7.
- **F-029 Activation legitimately exceeds 100% and the colour scale must not clamp.** Farber et al. 2009 reports amateur trail-arm pronator teres at **120.9% MMT**; Marta et al. 2012 notes wrist-flexor activity "above the maximal voluntary contraction". Dynamic ballistic EMG routinely exceeds a static isometric or manual-test reference. Separately, the widely-quoted four-tier band (<20 low / 20–40 moderate / 40–60 high / >60 very high) **could not be traced to any golf EMG paper** — the golf literature uses either a binary 30% MMT cutoff (Pink, Perry & Jobe 1993), raw percentages with no bands (Glazebrook 1994), or purely qualitative words (Kao 1995, McHardy 2005, Marta 2012). **Consequence:** T-021 must allow >100% without saturating, or it will silently truncate the single highest-activation muscle in the swing; and any banding scheme shipped must be attributed to general sports-EMG convention, not to a golf source. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md` §8.
- **F-030 Grip: the two hands do mechanically different jobs, and "grip force" is not one curve.** The lead hand supplies the dominant share of *linear grip force* throughout (last three fingers especially; Langlais & Broker 2014), while the trail hand supplies roughly **threefold more torque** (Choi & Park 2020). Choi also measured lead-arm force/torque onset and peak **preceding the trail arm by ~50–200 ms**, with trail peaks clustering at impact. Lead-hand grip signature is far more club-invariant than trail (r²=0.90 vs 0.73). Whether pressure *spikes* at impact is unsettled: Langlais & Broker find variability peaking during acceleration and collapsing at impact (convergence, not spike), and their trail index-finger spike appeared in only 4 of 8 golfers. **Consequence:** model lead and trail grip separately; do not animate a universal sharp impact spike as established. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md` §6.
- **F-031 Shoulder activation is single-sourced; forearm activation is multiply-sourced.** Every review from 2004–2025 re-cites the same Kerlan-Jobe fine-wire dataset collected 1986–1995 (n=7–15 per study, one lab) for the shoulder and scapula — it has never been replicated. The forearm has been re-measured repeatedly since (Farber 2009, Sorbie 2016/2017, Robinson 2023, Bochnia 2024, Grieß 2026). Also confirmed: **there is no Jobe/Perry/Pink elbow paper** — the Kerlan-Jobe golf series covers shoulder, scapula, trunk, hip and knee only. **Consequence:** the app's confidence indicator (if any) should rate shoulder values lower than forearm values, despite the shoulder numbers looking more complete. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md` §10.
- **F-032 Amateurs work the forearm harder and earlier than professionals — the only replicated skill-level upper-limb finding.** Two independent studies agree: Farber 2009 (fine-wire, %MMT) found amateurs over-recruit *trail* pronator teres in forward swing (120.9% vs 57.4%, p=.04) while professionals recruit *lead* pronator teres more in acceleration (88.1% vs 36.3%, p=.03) — the direction **reverses by arm**; Sorbie 2016 found amateurs produced significantly greater forearm activity than professionals in both backswing and downswing across all grip sizes (p<0.05). **No pro-vs-amateur comparison exists for any shoulder-girdle muscle** — Marta 2012 states the gap directly. Note Jobe 1989 is men-vs-women *professionals*, not skill level, and its differences were **not statistically significant**. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md` §9.
- **F-026 Published %MMT values pool fine-wire and surface electrodes silently.** McHardy tabulates values from both techniques together without distinguishing them. Surface electrodes cannot validly record supraspinatus or subscapularis at all. **Consequence:** cross-muscle magnitude comparisons within a single published table are provisional, even before the cross-study unit problem in F-010.
- **F-033 Third-pass independent verification upgrades §1A/F-027 from single-source to directly confirmed; softens Kim 2004's status; corrects one misattribution.** A third session re-fetched McHardy & Pollard 2005's actual PDF (PMC1725059, open access) and read it in full: Table 3, Table 4, the five-phase definitions, the handedness/handicap/flexor-burst quotes, and even a print error (infraspinatus values appear in Table 3 but the muscle is missing from Table 2's inventory) all match the earlier extraction **exactly, byte-for-byte**. Cross-checking F-027's "full Pink 1990 grid" against this freshly-verified table finds **20 of 20 checkable cells match exactly** (not the "9/9" first claimed — the scapular-table overlap wasn't counted the first time). Also independently verified via Europe PMC's abstract API: **Jobe, Moynes & Antonelli 1986**'s full abstract (correct title "Rotator cuff function during a golf swing"; states the deltoid was tested bilaterally and found inactive on **both** sides, not trail-only as F-027's framing implied; states rotator cuff activity overall and lat dorsi/pec major power are **bilateral**, per the primary source's own words) — this resolves §2.1/§2.3/§2.9's flagged uncertainty about lead/trail asymmetry for these three muscles, and explains the pec major 93%/93% "anomaly" mechanistically: same amplitude, but trail contracts concentrically and lead contracts eccentrically (McHardy's Discussion, verbatim). Also confirmed **Escamilla & Andrews 2009** as the correct primary source for the 28–68%/~70%/42–52%/~60%/~60% MVIC figures — a separate research pass had misattributed these to McHardy & Pollard. **Kim et al. 2004's ordinal table remains genuinely unverified** — three independent access attempts across two sessions (13 total routes) confirm it is paywalled with no accessible full text; given the same research lineage's demonstrated accuracy on every independently-checkable claim, treat it as probably genuine but formally unconfirmed, not as suspect. **Consequence:** T-012/T-021 can treat §1A's shoulder grid with materially higher confidence than F-027 originally stated. Source: `docs/research/04-shoulder-arm-forearm-and-grip.md` §1B.

- **F-027 The full Pink 1990 shoulder grid was recovered, upgrading seven zones.** Complete %MMT values, both sides, all five phases, for pectoralis major, subscapularis, supraspinatus, infraspinatus and anterior deltoid — **cross-validated 20/20 against McHardy's independently verified Table 3**. Supraspinatus, all three deltoid heads and others move from tier B to tier A. T-012's tier counts revised: A now 30 (51%), B 10, C 1, D 18 (30%). Source: `docs/research/07-verbatim-source-tables.md`.
- **F-028 Pectoralis major reads 93% on BOTH sides simultaneously — and this is real.** Confirmed against the primary PDF, not a transcription error. Trail contracts concentrically (drives rotation); lead contracts eccentrically at the same amplitude (brakes it). **Consequence: identical colour, opposite mechanical role.** This is a hard limit on what colour intensity alone can express, and raises a genuine design question — should the app distinguish concentric from eccentric work? Affects T-021 and T-028.
- **F-029 The organising pattern of the whole upper body: trail peaks early, lead peaks late.** Holds for supraspinatus, infraspinatus, middle trapezius, anterior deltoid and wrist extensors. Infraspinatus is the clearest — trail 27% at back swing falling to 7%, lead climbing to 61% at early follow-through. The trail side positions and drives; the lead side stabilises then decelerates. Corroborated by injury epidemiology: **over 90% of golfers' shoulder injuries are lead-side**, ~3× the trail rate.
- **F-030 Middle and posterior deltoid are quantitatively non-contributory** — 2–8% and 5–24%, no pattern across phases. Jobe 1986: deltoid inactive on the trail side throughout, and on the lead side except a brief anterior spurt in the milliseconds before contact. **This is content, not a gap** — two large, prominent shoulder muscles that stay cold are exactly the contrast the user asked to preserve.
- **F-031 Confidence is not uniform across the body.** Shoulder/scapular data is single-lab, single-era, never replicated (Kerlan-Jobe 1986–1995, n=7–15). The forearm has five independent modern replications. **Consequence:** a tier-A shoulder value and a tier-A forearm value do not carry equal weight; the uncertainty treatment in T-028 must encode replication count, not just data availability.
- **F-032 Triceps and brachioradialis are trail-measured, lead-unmeasured** — not wholly absent as F-018 implied. Pezarat-Correia 2006 (n=3, conference proceedings, indicative only): trail triceps 50% MVC peaking downswing; trail brachioradialis 45% MVC peaking backswing. Lead side of both has no data. **Consequence:** these zones need per-side tiers, correcting the earlier all-D assignment.
- **F-033 Kim et al. 2004's table content remains formally unverified.** This session verified the *citation* against PubMed (PMID 15262661 — real). The peer research session could not access the paper itself despite 13 attempts across two sessions. **Consequence:** the scapular grid in F-021 is probably genuine given the lineage's demonstrated accuracy, but is single-source and unconfirmed — label it as such rather than treating it as verified.

- **F-036 Back-solving muscle activation from observed golf-swing motion is mathematically under-determined and has never been validated for golf.** T-031 (`docs/research/09-inverse-activation-estimation.md`, 19 sources) found: (1) at any instant, joint moments recovered by inverse dynamics can be produced by infinitely many muscle-force combinations (redundancy problem — e.g. a 43-muscle leg model resolves only ~6 leg degrees of freedom), so any single back-solved activation pattern is *a* solution consistent with a chosen cost function or EMG constraint, never *the* solution; motor-control literature (Latash 2012) further argues even one golfer's repeated swings may not reuse one fixed pattern. (2) Static-optimisation agreement with real EMG ranges from r/R²≈0.0–0.97 depending on muscle and task — best for slow, cyclic, group-averaged, uni-articular-extensor cases (walking gait: mean r=0.74, Michaud et al. 2021); worst for fast/individual-level/biarticular cases (hopping: tibialis anterior R²=0.02 individual-level; Jessup et al. 2023). (3) Accuracy degrades specifically as tasks become faster/more dynamic (paraspinal XCorr collapses from ≥0.97 quasi-static to 0.51 walking/running, Rauber et al. 2024; predictive gait simulation fails qualitatively at the walk-run transition, Afschrift et al. 2025) — no study anywhere validates any of these methods at golf-downswing speed (~0.25 s). (4) EMG-informed models only modestly beat pure optimisation for normal movement (~9% RMSE reduction) but become close to necessary for idiosyncratic/"sub-optimal" movement relative to the cost function — exactly the category an individual golf swing falls into. (5) No muscle-level (static-optimisation/CMC/EMG-driven) model of the golf swing exists in the literature; the only golf-specific dynamics models (Nesbit 2005 ×2, Bae et al. 2014) compute net joint torques/loads only, not muscle forces, none EMG-validated, and Nesbit's own paper states "how well these loads represent actual subject joint loads is not known." - **F-037 The oblique axial-rotation rule is now established and cited, and it dictates the app's entire lead/trail rotation colour pattern.** External oblique unilateral contraction produces CONTRALATERAL trunk rotation (right EO → rotates trunk left); internal oblique unilateral contraction produces IPSILATERAL rotation (right IO → rotates trunk right); the two work as a cross-body pair, e.g. right EO + left IO co-contract to rotate the trunk left (Wikipedia "Abdominal external/internal oblique muscle" articles, directly quoting the anatomical convention and a worked example of the opposite pairing). For a right-handed golfer, the downswing rotates the torso toward the target (leftward), driven by trail (right) external oblique + lead (left) internal oblique — consistent with this project's own EMG finding that trail external oblique peaks in late downswing/acceleration (`02-trunk-and-core-activation.md`). Erector spinae unilaterally rotates to the SAME side (matches internal oblique's sign); multifidus unilaterally rotates to the OPPOSITE side despite also being an extensor (matches external oblique's sign) — the two deep-back "extensor" muscles do not share a rotation sign with each other. **Consequence:** T-021/T-035 can now assign rotation direction correctly per muscle, not just per side. Moment arms for the trunk rotators themselves could not be recovered in numeric cm/mm form — the primary sources (Bogduk 1992, Stokes & Gardner-Morse 1999, Christophy 2012) are paywalled with no PMCID across repeated targeted searches — so rotational leverage ranking (obliques > multifidus/erector spinae) is stated qualitatively (fibre geometry/moment-arm distance from the spinal axis) but not numerically confirmed; flagged as an open gap for a future access route (T-035/library pass). Numeric moment arms were recovered elsewhere with clear joint-angle dependence: knee biceps femoris long head peaks 3.0 cm at 55° flexion, knee extensor mechanism peaks 4.7 cm at 25° flexion (Arnold et al. 2010, PMC2903973); hip rotation moment arms change enough with hip flexion angle that several of 18 muscle compartments switch rotation direction outright (Delp et al. 1999) — a fixed "muscle X does action Y" mapping is invalid for hip rotators away from neutral, directly relevant to the flexed/internally-rotated trail hip through most of the swing. Six muscles in the zone list are biarticular (rectus femoris, biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus, gastrocnemius, biceps/triceps brachii long heads) and their net joint action cannot be inferred from activation alone without knowing the angle at BOTH joints crossed and co-contracting synergists — Lombard's paradox (a biarticular muscle can extend a joint it anatomically flexes, via a co-contracted antagonist) is the sharpest citable proof of this (Schumacher et al. 2020, PMC7061696). Eccentric contraction compounds the problem: a muscle active while lengthening produces motion OPPOSITE its concentric action, and can do so at the SAME OR LOWER EMG amplitude than the equivalent concentric contraction (Paschalis et al. 2026) — directly explains this project's own F-028 finding that lead/trail pectoralis major read identically (93%/93%) while performing opposite mechanical jobs (trail concentric drive, lead eccentric brake — McHardy & Pollard 2005 Discussion), and is independently corroborated for throwing-sport shoulder/elbow deceleration (Trasolini et al. 2022) and for golf gluteus medius during the backswing (Edwards et al. 2020). Force-velocity and force-length effects further decouple activation from force: eccentric force rises above isometric maximum with lengthening velocity in a biphasic curve, and starting muscle length measurably changes the rate of force development (Kissane & Askew 2026) — meaning the same %MVC/%MMT number can correspond to substantially different force and motion depending on swing phase. Source: `docs/research/12-functional-anatomy-and-moment-arms.md`.

- **F-038 The oblique rotation rule is VERIFIED against peer-reviewed primary literature.** Chino K, Ando R, Suzuki Y. "Verification of surface electromyographic activity of the oblique externus abdominis using ultrasound shear wave elastography." *Physiological Reports* 2022;10(9):e15295, PMC9069374, open access. States verbatim: **"The OE is a contralateral axial rotator"** and **"the OI… is an ipsilateral axial rotator."** During right trunk rotation the right OI is agonist and the right OE antagonist; during left rotation the right OE is agonist. **Consequence for a right-handed golfer:** the downswing rotates the trunk leftward (toward target), so the drivers are **trail (right) external oblique + lead (left) internal oblique**. Independently corroborated by this project's own EMG: Marta 2013 shows trail EO peaking at 59–67% EMGmax in late downswing — consistent only if trail EO is a contralateral (leftward) rotator. This supersedes the Wikipedia-sourced version in `12-functional-anatomy-and-moment-arms.md`. The entire lead/trail torso colour pattern depends on this and it is now settled.
- **F-039 Surface EMG over the external oblique is substantially contaminated by internal oblique — this degrades our own oblique data.** Same paper (Chino 2022): subcutaneous tissue plus OE overlying the OI measures only 1.8 ± 0.3 cm, well within surface-electrode detection range. During right trunk rotation, EMG amplitude at the right OE electrode reached **61 ± 30%** of its left-rotation amplitude — i.e. the electrode reads large "agonist" signal when OE should be antagonist, because it is picking up the OI beneath. **Consequence:** the trail-EO figure of 59–67% EMGmax (Marta 2013, surface EMG) may be partly internal oblique. Downgrade oblique confidence and label it. Adds a fifth error class on top of F-010/F-016's unit incompatibility: **anatomical crosstalk**.
- **F-040 No muscle-driven forward-dynamics simulation of a golf swing exists anywhere.** Confirmed by targeted searching: every golf muscle study runs motion → muscle force, never activation → motion. The technique itself is proven outside golf — Rajagopal 2016 (gait, 3% RMSE vs inverse dynamics), Remus 2023 (muscle-driven spine, sagittal plane only), Lee et al. 2019 SIGGRAPH (346-muscle full-body model driven by a trained deep-RL policy producing realistic gait). None golf-validated. Source: `docs/research/08-musculoskeletal-simulation.md` (36 sources).
- **F-041 Musculoskeletal simulation cannot run on a phone — it must be precomputed offline.** Every published performance figure is far below real time: 11×–18× real time for plain forward dynamics on a shoulder model; up to ~4,700× real time for computed muscle control on an upper-limb model; 10 minutes to 4 hours per simulation run, all on desktop hardware. **Consequence:** confirms the architecture — simulate offline, ship baked animation plus activation curves as data. This is a settled design constraint, not an open question.
- **F-042 Trunk axial rotation is adequately modelled in only two published models.** A 2025 systematic review (Carpenedo et al.) rates the two most widely reused lumbar models — Christophy 2012 and Bruno 2015 — as having only "Limited" axial rotation. The exceptions: **Full-Body Lumbar Spine model** (Beaucage-Gauvreau 2019; 21 segments, ~30 DOF, 324 muscles, 5 individually modelled lumbar vertebrae) and Ignasiak 2016. Neither has ever been used for a golf swing. Rajagopal 2016 has trunk degrees of freedom but drives them with idealised torque actuators, not muscles. **Consequence:** no off-the-shelf model fits; T-014 requires grafting a trunk model onto separate limb and shoulder models.
- **F-043 Licence position is favourable but not yet verified word-for-word.** OpenSim core and Moco are Apache-2.0. The Seth 2019 thoracoscapular shoulder model states "any research or commercial use without restrictions" — the most permissive found. **Risks:** the Full-Body Lumbar Spine model is split across two differently-licensed bundles (CC BY 4.0 vs a custom agreement, must be checked per file), and MoBL-ARMS appears to carry a Non-Commercial clause. Several licence texts were seen only as page labels, not read in full. **Blocks any shipping decision until verified** — we are shipping a free public app (D-006), but "free" does not automatically satisfy a non-commercial clause.

- **F-044 Zero golf EMG exists for any deep calf or intrinsic foot muscle — and the gap is in the primary literature, not our searching.** Nothing for tibialis posterior, flexor hallucis longus, flexor digitorum longus, popliteus, peroneus brevis, or any intrinsic foot muscle. A 24-study systematic review (Sports Medicine 2026, PMC13198453) independently confirms none of its included studies discussed foot musculature or ankle kinematics at all. No study anywhere separates peroneus brevis from longus in any rotational or weight-shifting task. Source: `docs/research/13-foot-ankle-and-plantar-pressure.md` (26 sources).
- **F-045 Plantar pressure gives real numbers, and independently corroborates two figures we already held.** Pataky et al. 2015 (PMID 25010946, N=32): peak plantar pressures 0–200+ kPa; **lateral target-forefoot pressure correlates with clubhead speed at r=0.632, p<0.001**. Highest lead-foot pressures at lateral forefoot, heel and hallux; highest trail-foot at medial forefoot and hallux. Okuda et al. 2010 gives lead-foot vertical GRF ~90–113% bodyweight at impact, independently corroborating our ~95% figure from Chu 2010. Shoe-torque studies report lead-foot torque of **17–19 Nm**, matching our free-moment figure almost exactly. Two independent measurement routes agreeing is the strongest evidence pattern available.
- **F-046 The foot defines the legitimate/illegitimate inference boundary precisely — use it as the template for all Stage 6 work.** LEGITIMATE: pressure, torque and GRF data prove *that* large, phase-varying, laterally asymmetric forces pass through specific foot regions, and that passive tissue alone cannot account for it (Farris et al. 2020, windlass mechanism) — therefore some muscle combination must generate and resist it. ILLEGITIMATE: deriving any %MVC value or activation-timing curve for a named foot muscle from that data. Pressure insoles and force plates measure net external mechanical consequence, not muscle activation, and no calibrating EMG study exists to bridge them.

- **F-047 There IS golf-specific deep-core data, from exactly one group.** Skibski et al., UCF READY Lab (2020/2022/2024; PMID 33059233, 36094264, 38329285): real-time ultrasound of transversus abdominis and the obliques in golfers with low-back-pain history, **one study measuring the obliques during an actual swing**. This is the only golf deep-core measurement in existence. TrA was measured statically only. Source: `docs/research/14-deep-core-and-intra-abdominal-pressure.md` (67 sources).
- **F-048 The intra-abdominal pressure mechanism is well quantified outside golf, and supports legitimate inference.** Diaphragm + TrA + pelvic floor + abdominal wall pressurise the cavity, stiffening the spine and unloading it via a direct extensor moment of **~6 Nm from pressure alone** (Hodges 2001, PMID 11182126). Phrenic-nerve-stimulated IAP rise of 27–61% increased lumbar stiffness **8–31%** (Hodges 2005, PMID 16023475). Voluntary IAP at 40%/80% of max raised flexion stiffness **21%/42%** and lateral-bend stiffness **16%/30%** (Cholewicki 1999, PMID 10552322). Lifting/Valsalva IAP measured at **79–277 mmHg** by task (Harman 1988; Blazek 2019). **Legitimate inference route:** the diaphragm fires ~20 ms *before* the deltoid in feedforward postural tasks, coincident with TrA onset (Hodges 1997, PMID 9423192), and the same feedforward architecture extends to psoas/QL/TrA/multifidus together (Yamane 2022) — this supports inferring deep-core pre-activation ahead of the downswing. Strongest golf-relevant link: TrA shows reciprocal left/right activity specifically during twisting, "indicating an ability for torque development" (Cresswell 1992, PMID 1534959). Beyond this, any specific golf IAP value, timing curve or amplitude is pure extrapolation.
- **F-049 Some gaps may be permanent, not merely unfilled.** TrA, multifidus and diaphragm require fine-wire or intramuscular EMG; IAP requires intragastric or intravesical catheterisation; pelvic floor requires intravaginal or intra-anal electrodes. **All are incompatible with a full-speed unrestricted golf swing.** Surface ultrasound (the Skibski approach) is the only viable non-invasive compromise and has only reached the oblique layer dynamically. **Consequence:** for these zones the never-measured render state is not a temporary condition awaiting future research — it is likely permanent, and the app should not imply the data is merely pending.
- **F-050 The literature quantifies spinal stiffness in the two directions that do not matter for golf, and not the one that does.** Flexion and lateral-bend stiffness are well quantified (see F-048). **No numeric value for rotational/torsional spinal stiffness (Nm/rad) exists anywhere in the deep-core literature** — despite axial rotation being the golf swing's actual demand. Also absent: any breathing-pattern, Valsalva or breath-hold measurement during golf, or during any comparable rotational striking or throwing sport (baseball pitch, tennis serve).

- **F-055 The lumbar rotation limit is a hard rigging constraint, and explains golf back injury.** Total lumbar axial rotation is **≈5–9° across L1–S1** (≈1–2° per motion segment; White & Panjabi 1990 cadaveric data). Whole-torso rotation amplitude reaches ~129° and X-factor alone 48–57°. **Consequence for T-014:** the rig must route nearly all visible "trunk turn" through the **thoracic spine, hips, and pelvis-against-feet** — a rig that distributes rotation evenly along the spine would be anatomically impossible. Independently corroborated by golf injury-mechanism literature, which treats excess lumbar rotation as a compensation pattern for restricted hip mobility. This also gives the app a genuinely useful teaching point.
- **F-056 No open 3D golf motion-capture dataset exists.** GolfDB (McNally et al. 2019) is the only public golf dataset: **2D video only, CC BY-NC 4.0**, built for swing-event detection rather than joint angles, and unusable for our purpose without an unvalidated 2D→3D lifting step. **Licence warning:** CC BY-NC is Non-Commercial; per F-043 a free app does not automatically satisfy an NC clause — this needs the same word-for-word review as the model licences (T-040). CMU's general mocap database could not be checked (TLS certificate failure on every attempt) — status unconfirmed, not ruled out. **Consequence:** the swing animation must be keyframed from published joint-angle values, not driven by a downloaded motion file.
- **F-057 Both elbows are a genuine literature gap.** No absolute elbow angle at any named swing event, either side — only amplitude and range figures. One paper whose title implies elbow data reports none in its results. Also poorly documented: trail shoulder at named events (pooled amplitude only), address-position hip and knee flexion, and **head position/stability**, which has almost nothing — one unverified secondhand rotational figure and no translation or sway data at all. **Consequence:** the lead arm's "straightness" and the trail elbow fold — two of the most visually obvious features of a swing — must be keyframed from range data plus anatomical constraint, and labelled as such.
- **F-058 The kinematics literature has the same independence problem as the EMG literature.** Severin 2022, Zhou 2022 and Steele 2018 reuse the same underlying professional cohort across papers. Three citations, one measurement of the world. This independently validates F-051's lineage requirement and confirms it must apply to the kinematic dataset as well as the activation dataset, not just to EMG.

- **F-060 Video fails precisely where golf needs it, on all three axes at once.** (1) **Speed** — every validated markerless task (walking, running, squatting, jumping) is slower than a golf downswing; OpenCap drop-jump knee RMSE exceeds 6° with the authors stating it "cannot be recommended", and jump-landing peak-force bias exceeds 40%. (2) **Rotation** — transverse/rotational accuracy is worse than sagittal in *every* matched comparison found: Theia3D internal/external rotation limits of agreement ±15.75° vs ±10.71° for flexion/extension; transverse-plane range 3–57° vs sagittal 3–15°. (3) **Impact** — video-derived kinetics degrade most at ballistic impact, exactly golf's defining event. Video is acceptable only for slow sagittal-plane flexion/extension (RMSE <5–6°; pooled OpenCap 4.94° after correction, r=0.845, meta-analysis of 12 studies / 184 participants, PMID 41783455). Source: `docs/research/11-video-pose-estimation.md` (47 sources).
- **F-061 Trunk axial rotation cannot be recovered from video, and the only golf markerless study says so itself.** No study anywhere isolates a trunk-axial-rotation RMSE, in golf or otherwise. Yamamoto et al. 2023 (PMID 38033658), the sole golf-specific markerless-pose study, used a 240 Hz camera, **never attempted rotation measurement**, and states its single-camera method is "inferior… compared with 3D motion analysis" specifically because "the golf swing is a 3D motion involving rotation and twisting". Monocular 3D pose surveys name depth ambiguity as an unsolved "fundamental challenge" (PMID 40285099). A 24-study golf GRF/CoP systematic review contained **zero** markerless studies. **Consequence:** video is ruled out as an evidence source for the single most important movement in the swing. It remains usable for event timing only.
- **F-062 CaddieSet is MIT-licensed and therefore usable, unlike GolfDB.** CaddieSet (Jung et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.20491): 924–1,757 swings (source-internal discrepancy unresolved), 2D HRNet keypoints, event detection 78.0%/94.1%, **MIT licence — compatible with a free public app**. GolfDB by contrast is CC BY-NC 4.0 and additionally samples at 30 fps, where its own authors note "at 30 fps it was rare that the precise moment of impact was captured". OpenCap is Apache 2.0 and licence-compatible, but has never been validated on golf or any comparably fast rotational task. **Consequence:** CaddieSet is the only licence-clean golf video asset found, and its value is swing-event timing, not joint angles.

- **F-063 The >100% MVC problem is a measurement-definition artefact, not a physiological claim — and the user's objection is well founded.** The user is correct that a muscle cannot exceed its own maximum. Published values above 100% (erector spinae 83–106%, amateur pronator teres 120.9%) arise because the denominator — a maximum voluntary *isometric* contraction measured standing still — underestimates what the muscle produces in a fast dynamic movement. The muscle is not exceeding itself; the reference is too small. **Resolution (supersedes F-001's open question):** renormalise every muscle to the maximum it actually reaches during the golf swing, so the scale runs 0–100% of that muscle's real swing maximum. This removes the >100% artefact entirely, matches the user's mental model, and is more useful to a golfer than a laboratory reference. Requires the renormalisation to be documented and the underlying laboratory values retained in the database (never destroyed).

- **D-016a Refinement (user, 2026-08-07): eccentric lengthening is emergent, not an input.** User's argument: "stretching itself is a result of the muscle itself firing or contracting, or other muscles connected to it directly or indirectly causing that muscle to stretch — so this shouldn't matter?" **Assessment: correct for the app.** Given a complete activation map across all muscles over time plus fixed initial conditions, contraction mode is determined output, not additional information requiring specification. The app therefore needs to store and display activation and timing only — contraction mode is never a field the user sees or the data model must carry as an input. **Narrow remaining exception, internal only:** during data validation, a record showing high activation while the joint moves against that muscle's pull must not be flagged as contradictory and discarded — it is a valid eccentric record. This is a rule about not throwing away good evidence, not about adding a data dimension. Sent to T-055 for rigorous verification, including whether momentum, gravity and passive elastic tissue constitute lengthening causes not attributable to any concurrently contracting muscle.

- **F-064 D-016 verified: the user is correct for the instruction model, and contraction mode stays internal.** Forward-dynamics determinism confirmed — given activation history plus initial conditions, contraction mode is emergent output, not an input (Xie et al. 2026, PMC12977398). **However, activation alone is not the complete input set**: four non-activation inputs exist — momentum/interaction torque, gravity, contact force, and passive elastic tissue. Hirashima et al. 2009 (PMC2777815) show elbow extension driven by interaction torque transmitted from trunk rotation, with the elbow "passively complying" and no elbow-crossing muscle causing it. Tendon recoil returned **59% of positive work** in a running model (Schroeder & Kuo 2021, PMC8651147). **Our own golf data demonstrates the temporal decoupling**: trail subscapularis (64%) and lead infraspinatus (61%) switch concentric→eccentric at early follow-through, decelerating momentum created by *different muscles that fired earlier and are no longer contracting*. **Verdict:** contraction mode is never a user-facing field (user correct), but must be retained per record in the internal pipeline or force estimates go systematically wrong post-impact (user's "discard it entirely" is incorrect). Source: `docs/research/15-contraction-states-and-motor-control.md` (30+ sources).
- **F-065 A person cannot consciously fire an individual muscle — this constrains how the app must present itself.** Hug et al. 2023 (PMC10098498): "selective volitional control of single motor neurons… cannot be achieved", even with biofeedback. The CNS commands roughly 5–6 muscle synergies, not individual muscles (Monte et al. 2024). Separately, conscious attention to body parts **degrades** fast skilled movement, and does so *more* in skilled performers — meta-analysis SMD 0.32–0.59 favouring external focus (Zang et al. 2025, PMC12424610), with a golf putting study among the evidence. **Consequence:** the app's value is pre- and post-swing understanding and training, **not** real-time in-swing cueing. This does not invalidate D-016's instruction model, but it does mean the app should not present itself as "fire this muscle now, mid-swing". Needs a user decision on framing.
- **F-066 Eccentric force is roughly double isometric at the same activation.** Single-fibre evidence (Lombardi & Piazzesi 1990 via Herzog 2018, PMC6189250); ~4–5× effort-matched mechanical output difference (Abbott et al. 1952). Since the app displays activation/exertion rather than force (user's explicit spec), this is moot at the display layer — but it is decisive upstream when deriving activation from torque, and is the reason F-064 requires contraction mode in the pipeline.
- **F-067 Non-golf transfer is defensible, but only as a tiered method governed by Dynamic Correspondence.** Verkhoshansky's framework (formalised PMID 36412764) requires matching movement amplitude/direction, force-production region, force-velocity dynamics, timing of peak force, and contraction regime. Each mismatch measurably breaks transfer, and recruitment can differ *within the same named muscle* by task even at matched joint torque (PMIDs 42031565, 42287257, 41615326) — so "same muscle, same task type" is necessary but not sufficient. **Four-tier rulebook:** Tier 0 golf mechanical proof without EMG attribution; Tier 1 near-identical rotational/ballistic task (licenses magnitude bands and cross-muscle ranking, never exact %MVC transplant); Tier 2 shared mechanical demand, different task (presence and coarse phase only); Tier 3 generic anatomical function (existence and plausibility only, zero magnitude). A record that cannot state which criteria matched is **excluded**, not defaulted to Tier 3. Source: `docs/research/16-non-golf-inference-transfer.md`.
- **F-068 Three high-value transferable datasets found, ice hockey being the closest analogue to golf.** (1) **Ice hockey slap shot** (PMC11942957, n=21, true %MVIC) — unstable moving base, ballistic rotational strike; erector spinae **73.6%**, internal oblique **43.7%** MVIC on ice; the authors explicitly cite golf as the same mechanical class. (2) **Tennis serve trunk EMG** (Chow 2009, PMC2770553) — full 6-muscle × 3-serve-type × 2-skill × 4-phase grid; obliques peak in acceleration, internal oblique to 183% of trial peak, matching golf's own trail-EO acceleration pattern. (3) **Standing trunk-twisting** (PMC7727425, true %MVC) — multifidus 39–51% MVC scaling with lower-body loading, directly useful for Stage 4 constraint propagation between leg loading and deep spinal engagement.
- **F-069 Some muscles remain unfillable even with non-golf transfer.** Zero dynamic-task EMG exists anywhere, in any sport, for **piriformis, obturator internus/externus, quadratus femoris**. Zero EMG of any kind for **rotatores and semispinalis**. Neck muscles have only reflexive/passive-perturbation data, which fails Dynamic Correspondence on regime and context simultaneously. Three of the four muscle groups targeted returned nothing across multiple independent search strategies — **the ceiling is the published literature, not search effort.** Also: no study anywhere compares golf to another rotational sport in the same protocol and subjects, which would have been the single most valuable source.
- **F-070 Evidence database design settled: SQLite primary with DuckDB attached, append-only.** SQLite is the only candidate providing full constraint enforcement (CHECK, FK, STRICT typing) plus single-file portability plus zero server administration simultaneously; DuckDB's `sqlite_scanner` attaches to the live file for wide analytical scans with no ETL and no second copy of truth. PostgreSQL rejected (admin burden), Parquet rejected as primary (whole-file rewrite per correction, wrong for append-only corrections), plain JSON/CSV rejected as primary (no constraint enforcement) but retained as the git-diffable export. Lineage is a directed graph traversed by recursive CTE to count distinct roots. All measured values append-only with a supersession chain; corrections and Stage 4 reassignments audited, never destructive. Recovery floor: WAL mode, `VACUUM INTO` snapshots, plus a deterministic per-table CSV export tree committed to git. Full design: `docs/design/evidence-database-architecture.md` (1018 lines). **Stated risks:** some guarantees rest on sanctioned views/scripts rather than hard constraints; an undetected reproduction would inflate independent-origin counts and falsely promote sufficiency; the sufficiency cache goes stale if not recomputed after each ingestion batch.
