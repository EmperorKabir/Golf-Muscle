# Lower limb and hip activation, weight shift and ground reaction force

Phase abbreviations used below: ADD address, TA takeaway/early backswing, LB late backswing, TOP
top/transition, ED early downswing, LD/ACC late downswing–acceleration (pre-impact), IMP impact,
EFT early follow-through, LFT late follow-through. L = lead leg (left, right-handed golfer), T = trail
(right). **N/D = no data found.** No values are invented to fill gaps.

## Critical: the literature and the coaching world define "acceleration" differently

Primary EMG studies (Bechler 1995; Watkins 1996; Pink/Jobe/Perry lineage; Marta 2016) use a **5-phase
model**: takeaway, backswing, forward swing/downswing, **acceleration (impact → early follow-through)**,
follow-through. In that literature "acceleration" begins **at** impact.

Coaching and instructional sources (TPI and most golf media) use "acceleration phase" to mean the **final
part of the downswing before impact**.

These are opposite sides of impact. Any phase-averaged figure quoted as "acceleration" is ambiguous until
its source is checked. **The app must pick one convention explicitly and never mix them.** Recommend the
peer-reviewed definition internally, with user-facing labels written in plain language that avoids the word
entirely ("just before impact" / "just after impact").

## Per-muscle activation

### Gluteus maximus

| | ADD | TA | LB | TOP | ED | LD/ACC | IMP | EFT | LFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | Low | Low–mod | Low–mod | Rising | High | **Peak region** | High, declining | Mod | Low |
| Trail | Low | Low–mod, loading | Mod | Rising | High | **Peak** | High, declining | Declining | Low |

Bechler et al. 1995 (verified via PubMed abstract, PMID 7670971): trail hip extensors and abductors
together with **lead adductor magnus** jointly initiate pelvic rotation in the forward swing; lead
hamstrings stabilise a flexed lead knee as the base for that rotation. Peak hip and knee EMG occurs
**earlier** than peak trunk and shoulder EMG — the lower limb leads the proximal-to-distal chain.

The full numeric table from Bechler 1995 could **not** be verified (paywalled, no accessible reproduction).
Practitioner sources paraphrase trail glute max as reaching "almost 100% MVC" — **unverified**, sourced only
from non-peer-reviewed paraphrase. Do not use that figure as fact.

Magnitude proxy from a non-swing task (Ghigiarelli et al. 2014, golf-specific hip-rotation exercises, %MVIC):
trail glute max 36.4±16.2 (band-resisted) to 53.4±20.5 (medicine-ball throw); lead glute max 17.4±11.1 to
31.6±10.4. Exercise analogues, not in-swing values, but they establish a plausible 30–55% MVIC floor with
trail exceeding lead.

Isometric strength (Callaway et al. 2012, N=56, %bodyweight): low-handicap right 30.5 / left 30.6;
high-handicap right 21.9 / left 20.7 (p<0.001 throughout), correlating with peak pelvis rotation speed
(r=0.42–0.49).

### Gluteus medius

| | ADD | TA | LB | TOP | ED | LD/ACC | IMP | EFT | LFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | Low | Low–mod | Low–mod | Mod | Mod–high | Mod–high | Mod | Mod | Low |
| Trail | Low | Low–mod | Mod | Mod | Rising | **High** | High | **High** | Mod |

Exercise-analogue %MVIC (Ghigiarelli 2014, not in-swing): trail 33–50, lead 25–30, with no significant
lead/trail or exercise-type difference — unlike glute max. Isometric strength (Callaway 2012): low-handicap
30.2–30.5 %BW vs high-handicap 22.4–22.6 %BW (p<0.001).

A widely repeated figure of "59% MMT, most active right-sided muscle" traces only to search synthesis of
Bechler 1995, not to verified primary text. **Unverified.**

### Hamstrings — the strongest quantified lower-limb evidence

**Biceps femoris**

| | ADD | TA | LB | TOP | ED | LD/ACC | IMP | EFT | LFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | Low | Low | Mod | Mod | Mod | High | High | High | Mod |
| Trail | Low | Mod–high | High | High | High | **68–73% EMGmax** | High, declining | Mod | Low |

**Semitendinosus**

| | ADD | TA | LB | TOP | ED | LD/ACC | IMP | EFT | LFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | Low | Low | Mod | Mod | Mod | Mod–high | High | Mod | Mod |
| Trail | Low | Mod | Mod–high | High | High | **70–76% EMGmax** | High, declining | Mod | Low |

Marta et al. 2016 (J Sports Sci 34(8), PMID 26197882) is the strongest quantitative primary source: surface
EMG bilaterally across ten lower-limb muscles, pitching wedge / 7-iron / 4-iron, N=14 non-elite golfers.
**Trail semitendinosus (70–76%) and trail biceps femoris (68–73%) are the two highest lower-limb values in
the entire dataset**, both peaking in the forward swing. Activation was generally higher with the 4-iron
than the pitching wedge.

Semimembranosus was not separately distinguished — treat as inferred by analogy, not independently measured.

### Quadriceps

**Vastus lateralis** — Bechler 1995 reports peak ~88% MMT (secondary synthesis, side not certain,
**unverified against primary text**), described as one of the highest-activation muscles in that study.
Bilateral vastus lateralis is prominent through late downswing, impact and both follow-through sub-phases,
consistent with eccentric braking decelerating the body after impact — particularly the lead leg.

**Vastus medialis and rectus femoris** — measured by Marta 2016, and both showed a significant
pitching-wedge vs 4-iron difference (higher with the longer club), but **exact per-phase percentages were
not retrievable** (paywalled; Unpaywall confirms no open-access copy). Direction is sourced; magnitude is not.

### Adductor group

| | ADD | TA | LB | TOP | ED | LD/ACC | IMP | EFT | LFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | Low | Low | Low–mod | Mod | Rising | **High** | High | Mod–high | Mod |
| Trail | N/D | N/D | N/D | N/D | N/D | N/D | N/D | N/D | N/D |

Only **lead** adductor magnus is discussed functionally — initiating pelvic rotation together with trail hip
extensors/abductors (Chu et al. 2010, citing Bechler 1995). A peak of ~63% MMT is reported via secondary
synthesis only, **unverified**. No trail-side adductor data was located anywhere; that is unreported, not
zero. Adductor longus and brevis are not separately reported in any source found.

### Lower leg

**Gastrocnemius** — measured bilaterally by Marta 2016 (medial and lateral, not separately reported);
right gastrocnemius showed a significant club effect. Exact percentages not retrieved. Corroborated
functionally by inverse-dynamics data: ankle plantarflexor peak net joint moment lead 0.53±0.14 Nm/kg vs
trail 0.50±0.15 Nm/kg, no significant difference — but that is joint-moment data from an older-adult novice
sample, not EMG, and not necessarily generalisable.

**Tibialis anterior** — measured by Marta 2016; right side showed a significant club effect (higher with
longer club). Magnitude unverified. Expected pattern is postural and eccentric control, peaking trail-side
at transition as weight leaves the trail foot.

**Peroneus longus** — measured by Marta 2016; right side showed a significant club effect. Magnitude
unverified. Expected role is lateral foot stability during weight acceptance, lead-side-weighted through
downswing and impact.

**Soleus — no data.** No located study separates soleus from gastrocnemius. All lower-limb EMG protocols
found recorded gastrocnemius only.

### Muscles with no golf data at all

- **Gluteus minimus and the deep external rotators** (piriformis, obturators, gemelli, quadratus femoris) —
  too deep for surface EMG; no fine-wire golf study exists.
- **Tensor fasciae latae** — the only mention in the golf EMG literature is methodological: Ghigiarelli 2014
  note the examiner stabilised the pelvis "to prevent tensor fascia lata activity" during glute medius
  testing. It is treated as a confound to exclude, never measured.
- **Iliopsoas** — the gap is explicitly confirmed in print. Marta et al. 2012 state that "no available data
  were found concerning the iliopsoas, supposedly a powerful muscle playing an important role in mechanics
  during golf swing analysis", and that no golf study has used fine-wire electrodes on deep stabilisers
  (iliopsoas, transversus abdominis, multifidus). Fine-wire iliopsoas methodology exists in gait and
  rehabilitation work but **has never been applied to golf**.

## Weight shift, centre of pressure and ground reaction force

### Vertical ground reaction force (Chu, Sell & Lephart 2010; N=308, driver, dual force plates, mean handicap 8.4±8.4)

| Event (% of downswing) | Lead foot %BW | Trail foot %BW |
|---|---|---|
| Top of swing (0%) | 29.0 ± 12.1 | 64.5 ± 14.3 |
| Acceleration (66%) | 93.9 ± 28.5 | 46.4 ± 17.3 |
| 40 ms pre-impact (86%) | **95.1 ± 30.5 (peak)** | 41.0 ± 21.2 |
| Impact (100%) | 74.7 ± 29.7 | 35.5 ± 21.0 |

Downswing duration top→impact 0.30 ± 0.06 s; acceleration→impact only 0.10 ± 0.02 s.

**Peak lead-foot vertical force occurs before impact (~40 ms prior) and then declines into impact**,
attributed to an upward whole-body movement rather than a late reverse weight shift. Lead-foot *share* of
total vertical force stayed at 66–69% through that window despite the falling absolute magnitude.

### Skilled versus low-skilled (PMC3737954; N=13 skilled at 0.8±2.6 hcp vs N=17 low-skilled at 30.8±5.5, driver)

| Phase / foot | Skilled | Low-skilled |
|---|---|---|
| Backswing, trail foot | ~92 %BW | ~76 %BW |
| Downswing, lead foot | ~59 %BW | ~33 %BW |
| Impact, trail foot | ~38 %BW | ~25 %BW |

Skilled golfers load the trail foot earlier and more in the backswing, and transfer to the lead foot earlier
and more in the downswing. Timing, not just magnitude, is the discriminator.

### Rotational force couple

Barrentine, Fleisig, Johnson & Woolley 1994 (*Science and Golf II*, accessed via secondary synthesis — the
book chapter is paywalled): roughly 0.2 s after the top of the backswing, the trail foot pushes backward
and the lead foot pushes forward simultaneously, forming a horizontal couple that rotates the body toward
the target. Downswing durations reported: PGA 0.28 s, low-handicap 0.28 s, high-handicap 0.33 s — high
handicappers take **longer**, not shorter.

Free-moment torque (PubMed 24149910): lead foot ~17–19 Nm peak, ~40 Nm total across backswing plus
downswing; trail foot ~6–7 Nm generally, but **low-handicap golfers generated significantly more trail-foot
torque with a driver** (15.8–18.2 Nm depending on shoe) than medium or high handicaps (p<0.05).

### Centre of pressure trajectory (qualitative)

Address roughly central with a slight trail bias for driver; backswing migrates toward trail heel/midfoot;
transition reverses toward the lead foot **before the backswing is complete** — a ground-up initiation of
the kinematic sequence; impact predominantly under the lead foot and heel. Ball & Best 2007 identified
distinct CoP cluster strategies including a "front foot" group and a "reverse" group that shifts back toward
the trail foot after mid-downswing (cited via Chu 2010; original not accessed).

### Explicitly excluded as unreliable

One secondary figure ("skilled lead-foot 33±19 %BW vs less-skilled 59±28 %BW") **reverses** the direction of
every peer-reviewed source here. Flagged as likely transcription error or lead/trail confusion, and excluded
from the synthesis. TPI-circulated figures of ~199 %BW combined peak and ~140 %BW lead foot are directionally
consistent with the peer-reviewed data but come from a non-peer-reviewed industry source and could not be
verified.

## Which muscles peak, and when

| Muscle | Peak phase | Peak side | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semitendinosus | Forward swing | Trail (70–76% EMGmax) | **Strong — directly quantified** |
| Biceps femoris | Forward swing | Trail (68–73% EMGmax) | **Strong — directly quantified** |
| Gluteus maximus | Late downswing → acceleration | Trail dominant, lead close | Moderate — direction firm, magnitude unverified |
| Gluteus medius | Downswing → early follow-through | Trail | Moderate — magnitude only from exercise analogues |
| Adductor magnus | Early–late downswing | Lead | Moderate — direction confirmed, 63% MMT unverified |
| Vastus lateralis | Downswing → follow-through | Bilateral | Moderate — 88% MMT unverified; club effect confirmed |
| Rectus femoris, vastus medialis, gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior, peroneus longus | Downswing-weighted, club-length dependent | Not resolved | Weak–moderate — direction only |
| Soleus, gluteus minimus, TFL, deep rotators, iliopsoas | — | — | **No data** |

Lead-foot GRF peaks (~86% of downswing) fall in a similar window to peak lower-limb activation, supporting a
coupled muscular-and-ground-force mechanism — though **no located study co-registered EMG and GRF on the same
subjects**, so simultaneity is inferred, not demonstrated.

## Magnitude bands

| Band | Muscles | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Very high (≥65%) | Trail semitendinosus, trail biceps femoris | Marta 2016, directly measured |
| High (~50–65%) | Glute max (both sides), vastus lateralis, glute medius in rotational tasks | Bechler 1995 unverified specifics; Ghigiarelli 2014 exercise analogue |
| Moderate (~25–50%) | Adductor magnus, vastus medialis, rectus femoris, gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior, peroneus longus | Directional inference from Marta 2016 |
| Undetermined | Glute minimus, deep rotators, TFL, soleus, iliopsoas | No golf data |

For contrast: forearm flexors reportedly exceed 100% of isometric MVC in some studies (Marta 2012 review).
The lower limb, though highly active, does not reach the extreme relative levels seen distally — trail
hamstrings at ~75% are the lower-limb ceiling in the quantified literature.

## Skill differences

- ~70% of PGA Tour professionals initiate the downswing from the pelvis; professionals show pelvic
  deceleration before impact (energy transfer up the chain), largely absent or delayed in amateurs.
- X-factor and skill is **internally contested**: Cheetham et al. 2000 reportedly found no significant
  professional-versus-amateur difference, while Watanabe 1998, Myers 2008 and McLean 1992 found greater
  separation in better players. Disagreement noted within Chu et al. 2010's own discussion.
- Gluteal strength: low-handicap golfers significantly stronger in both glute max and medius bilaterally
  (p<0.001) with faster peak pelvis rotation (503°/s vs 380°/s, p<0.001), r=0.42–0.49 between the two
  (Callaway 2012).
- Amateurs' arm peak angular velocity precedes their thorax peak — arms used too early — with greater
  inter-trial variability, and club deceleration before impact where professionals continue accelerating.

**No study directly compares lower-limb EMG between professionals and amateurs during live swings.** All
skill differences above come from kinematics, kinetics or isometric strength.
