# 16 — Non-Golf Inference Transfer: Validating and Operationalising the Analogue-Transfer Strategy

Status: DONE 2026-08-07 (incremental write, single WebFetch-only session, no sub-agents per operating constraints, 23 sources)

**Bottom line:** the owner's non-golf-inference strategy is scientifically defensible as a *bounded, tiered* method, not as a blanket assumption. It is governed by the pre-existing "Dynamic Correspondence" framework (§1.1) plus direct EMG evidence that contraction mode, velocity and even within-muscle region break transfer when mismatched (§1.3–1.4). §2 turns this into a 4-tier rulebook. §3–4 harvest 23 non-golf sources against it, including the richest trunk-rotator EMG grids found anywhere in this project's research to date (tennis serve, ice hockey shooting). §5 confirms several muscle groups (diaphragm/pelvic floor/TrA at speed, deep hip rotators, rotatores/semispinalis, neck) remain unfillable to numeric precision by ANY current published method, golf or non-golf — the ceiling is in the literature itself, not in search effort.

## Purpose

Validates the project owner's claim: muscle usage for the ~66% of muscle-zones with no golf EMG data can be inferred by researching the same muscles performing their generic or task-shared function outside golf (e.g. diaphragm/core during forced breathing, deep hip rotators during pivoting in other sports), always with a golf-first source preference. This document (1) tests whether that strategy is scientifically defensible, (2) builds a graded transfer-validity rulebook, (3) harvests actual %MVC-style data for golf-unmeasured muscles from non-golf sources, (4) surveys rotational-sport EMG (the closest analogue tier), and (5) states which muscles remain unfillable even with transfer.

## 1. Is EMG transfer across tasks scientifically defensible? Literature on task-specificity

**Answer: conditionally yes, but only under a stated, testable set of matching conditions — never as a default assumption.** The literature converges on a specific framework (below) rather than a binary "transfer works / doesn't work" verdict; the owner's instinct is directionally correct but currently unbounded, and this section supplies the bounds.

### 1.1 The governing framework: Dynamic Correspondence (Verkhoshansky)

The most directly applicable existing framework is **"dynamic correspondence" (DC)**, created by Yuri Verkhoshansky in the early 1990s and formalised in strength-and-conditioning science (Source 1: PMID 36412764 / PMCID PMC9680266, *J Funct Morphol Kinesiol* 2022, open access narrative review). DC states that a training stimulus (or, by direct extension, an EMG/activation measurement) transfers to a target task **only to the degree that both share each of the following variables**:

1. **Amplitude and direction of movement** — range of motion and movement vector must match; the review notes force in sport is often applied through the ground, so closed-chain analogues transfer better than open-chain ones.
2. **Accentuated region of force production** — *where in the range of motion* peak force/activation occurs must correspond, not just the total range.
3. **Dynamics of effort** — the force-velocity profile (how force and speed trade off through the movement) must align.
4. **Rate and timing of maximum force production** — matching the sport's actual time-to-peak-force window (e.g. fast vs slow stretch-shortening cycle) is required; a mismatch invalidates transfer even if peak magnitude matches.
5. **Regime of muscular work** — contraction mode (concentric / eccentric / isometric / stretch-shortening cycle) must match; the review states plainly that "different adaptations take place between concentric and eccentric actions."
6. **Segmental interrelation** (added by Goodwin & Cleather, cited in the same review) — the joint-to-joint coordination pattern (which segments move together and in what order) must correspond, not just the prime-mover muscle.

The review explicitly documents **transfer failure modes**: open kinetic chain exercises "are not likely to transfer to the same degree even if the muscle groups used are similar"; elastic-band resistance increases strength/power metrics but shows "little evidence" of transfer to jump performance because it alters the force-production region; and even DC-compliant exercises fail to transfer if improperly sequenced in training. This gives a positive framework (what must match) and confirms that similarity of *prime mover muscle alone is insufficient* — a recurring risk in the owner's proposed strategy that must be guarded against explicitly.

### 1.2 EMG-specific evidence of non-transfer between contraction modes

Independent of the DC framework, direct EMG studies confirm that **the EMG-to-force relationship itself is not constant across contraction mode**, which is the more granular mechanism underlying DC criterion 5:

- Source 2 (Ruas et al., PMID 39367883, *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2025): during maximal knee extension, EMG-to-torque ratio was **lower for eccentric than for isometric and concentric** contractions — the same %MVC EMG signal corresponds to a different force depending on contraction mode.
- Source 3 (Desachy et al., PMID 40547839, *Front Aging Neurosci* 2025): "muscular activity was lower during eccentric than concentric contractions" at matched output.
- Source 4 (Haigney et al., PMID 40755446, *Eur J Neurosci* 2025): at low intensity (25% MVC) no significant excitability difference was found across isometric/concentric/eccentric actions — i.e. the divergence is itself load- and intensity-dependent, not a fixed offset, which rules out using a simple correction factor.

This directly corroborates the project's own existing finding (F-037, `12-functional-anatomy-and-moment-arms.md`): eccentric contraction can produce the *same or lower* EMG amplitude than concentric while performing the opposite mechanical role — already documented in golf for lead/trail pectoralis major (McHardy & Pollard 2005) and gluteus medius (Edwards et al. 2020). The new sources generalise this beyond golf, confirming it is a general neuromuscular property, not a golf-specific artefact — which strengthens (not weakens) the case that any transferred %MVC figure must be tagged with its source contraction mode and never silently equated with a different mode.

### 1.3 Velocity specificity

- Source 5 (meta-analysis, PMID 40839186, *Sports Med Open* 2025): eccentric-only isokinetic training improves eccentric MVC more than concentric or isometric MVC; **training/measuring at one velocity does not equally transfer to another**, and the effect shrinks further when training velocity and testing velocity diverge.
- Source 6 (PMID 40363107, *Sensors* 2025): the optimal EMG normalisation method itself varies by task and muscle, meaning cross-task EMG comparison additionally carries a *methodological* (not just physiological) transfer risk — pooling EMG data normalised differently across source studies (already flagged in this project as F-010/F-016) compounds the transfer problem when the sources being pooled are non-golf.

No source located gives a quantitative "decay function" for transfer as velocity mismatch increases; all evidence found is qualitative (transfer degrades, direction and rough magnitude confirmed, no interpolatable curve). This is stated as a limit, not filled by extrapolation.

### 1.4 Task-dependent recruitment within the *same* muscle

A further, more severe caveat: several 2025–2026 motor-unit studies show that **recruitment can differ regionally within one muscle depending on task, even at matched net joint torque** — meaning "same muscle, same force" is not by itself sufficient for transfer:

- Source 7 (PMID 42031565, *J Neurosci* 2026): 9/10 participants could voluntarily dissociate vastus medialis from vastus lateralis motor-unit activity; selective recruitment was possible specifically in proximal VM, not distal VM/VL — compartmentalised, task-dependent control within a single named "muscle."
- Source 8 (PMID 42287257, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2026): hamstring motor-unit discharge differed between hip-extension-dominant and knee-flexion-dominant tasks at matched force, with region-dependent (proximal vs distal) variability.
- Source 9 (PMID 41615326, *J Appl Physiol* 2026): soleus/medial/lateral gastrocnemius show independent directional tuning in single-leg stance that collapses to uniform activation in double-leg stance — the same triceps surae "muscle group" is task-fractionated.

**Consequence for the transfer rulebook:** even Tier 1 transfer (same muscle, near-identical task) should be read as applying to the muscle as a *named EMG electrode site*, not as a guarantee that every motor-unit pool/compartment within it behaves identically; this is a known residual uncertainty, not resolved by any source found.

### 1.5 Verdict on the owner's claim

The claim is **scientifically defensible as a research strategy, not as an assumption**. It is defensible because: (a) a peer-reviewed, decades-old framework (DC) exists specifically to define when cross-task transfer is valid, giving a non-arbitrary test rather than ad hoc judgement; (b) EMG/motor-control literature independently corroborates that mismatches in contraction mode, velocity, and even within-muscle region measurably break transfer, which means the framework's warnings are empirically grounded, not merely theoretical. It stops being defensible the moment a source is transferred without checking it against the DC criteria and without labelling the resulting record's confidence accordingly — i.e. "diaphragm is active in forced breathing, therefore diaphragm activity during the swing is X%" is illegitimate; "diaphragm shows feedforward pre-activation ahead of prime-mover onset in loaded postural tasks, and this feedforward architecture is shown to generalise across at least four different loaded/rotational tasks, therefore feedforward pre-activation timing (not magnitude) can be inferred for the swing, at reduced confidence" is legitimate. Section 2 formalises this into a graded rulebook.

## 2. The Transfer Rulebook

Built from the Dynamic Correspondence criteria (§1.1) applied specifically to muscle-activation (not training-adaptation) transfer, cross-referenced against this project's existing evidence-boundary finding (F-046: pressure/force data proves force passed through a region but cannot yield a named muscle's %MVC) and permanence finding (F-049: some muscles are unmeasurable by any method compatible with a full-speed swing). Every harvested record in §3–4 is tagged with its tier.

### Tier 1 — Same muscle, near-identical task (rotational/ballistic striking-throwing sports)
**Matches:** contraction regime (fast SSC), rate of force development, whole-body proximal-to-distal sequencing, axial-rotation dominance, ballistic single-effort structure. **Differs:** external implement, exact joint angles, sport-specific technique variance between athletes.
**Can justify:** approximate peak %MVC magnitude (with wide band), relative activation *ranking* across muscles in the kinetic chain, and phase-relative timing (e.g. "trunk rotators peak before shoulder rotators peak" ordering). **Cannot justify:** a precise numeric %MVC value transplanted as if golf-measured; exact timing in absolute milliseconds (golf's own swing duration and phase proportions differ from pitching/serving); direction-specific claims where anatomy of the analogue task differs (e.g. a two-handed bat swing vs one-handed golf grip changes forearm loading pattern).

### Tier 2 — Same muscle, shared mechanical demand, different task (weight-shift, single-leg stance, loaded trunk rotation, pivoting/cutting)
**Matches:** the specific mechanical *demand* class only (e.g. frontal-plane hip stabilisation during single-leg loading, or axial trunk stabilisation under rotational torque) — not the whole movement.
**Can justify:** *that* the muscle is active and *roughly which phase-type* recruits it (e.g. "active during weight transfer," "active during deceleration/stabilisation") — a presence/absence and coarse-phase claim only. **Cannot justify:** any specific %MVC magnitude carried over numerically, any fine-grained timing, or contraction-mode assumptions (per §1.2, a muscle stabilising eccentrically in a cutting task may show different EMG amplitude than the same muscle stabilising concentrically in golf's analogous phase).

### Tier 3 — Same muscle, generic function only (quiet standing, resting breathing, isolated joint-motion testing)
**Matches:** anatomical action and innervation only.
**Can justify:** *that the muscle exists and is capable of the mechanical role anatomy predicts* (mechanical necessity/anatomical obligation — the two legitimate inference routes already defined in T-048) — e.g. "the diaphragm participates in postural stabilisation feedforward of prime-mover onset in loaded tasks generally, therefore it plausibly does so in the golf swing too." **Cannot justify:** any numeric activation value, timing value, or amplitude at all. This is the tier golf-unmeasured muscles mostly fall into (§3), and it is the tier F-046's foot-muscle boundary describes: proof that force/demand exists, never a specific muscle's %MVC.

### Tier 0 (new, added by this document) — Mechanical/force evidence without EMG at all
Not EMG transfer but adjacent: GRF, pressure, torque, ultrasound-thickness or IAP data proving a mechanical demand exists in golf itself, without any muscle-level attribution. This is the existing F-046/F-048 category. Combining Tier 0 golf-specific mechanical proof with Tier 3 non-golf generic-function EMG is the *strongest available combination* for the permanently-unmeasurable muscles (§3.1) — it is still not measurement, but it is the most constrained inference route available, and should be labelled explicitly as "Tier 0+3 combined inference," never upgraded to look like a Tier 1 or 2 record.

### Rulebook usage rule
Every non-golf record entering the dataset schema (T-042) must carry: source task, tier (0/1/2/3), which DC criteria matched vs mismatched, and the specific claim type permitted (magnitude / ranking / phase-presence / existence-only). A record that cannot state which DC criteria matched does not get a tier — it is excluded, not defaulted to Tier 3, because Tier 3 itself requires the anatomical-action match to be explicit, not assumed.

## 3. Harvested non-golf data for golf-unmeasured muscles

### 3.1 Diaphragm, pelvic floor, transversus abdominis (TrA)

This project already holds substantial non-golf evidence for this group (F-048/F-049, `14-deep-core-and-intra-abdominal-pressure.md`, 67 sources): diaphragm firing ~20ms before deltoid onset in feedforward postural tasks (Hodges 1997, PMID 9423192), the same feedforward architecture extending to psoas/QL/TrA/multifidus together (Yamane 2022), TrA showing reciprocal left/right activity specifically during twisting (Cresswell 1992, PMID 1534959), and IAP-derived spinal stiffening of ~6 Nm (Hodges 2001) rising further under voluntary bracing (Cholewicki 1999). This document adds one new, task-relevant data point:

- **Source: Biswokarma et al. 2024, PMID 38487031, PMCID PMC10939065, *Front Med*, open access.** 22 asymptomatic women, transperineal ultrasound of pelvic-floor (urethrovesical junction) displacement plus intravaginal IAP transducer during a **ballistic isometric head-lift task** (a fast, forceful, whole-body-braced effort — not pelvic floor exercise). Pelvic floor displaced **6.0 ± 1.6 mm** (menstruation) / **5.1 ± 1.5 mm** (ovulation) during the unprepared ballistic lift, dropping to 3.9/3.4 mm with pelvic-floor pre-contraction — i.e. pre-activation measurably stiffens the response even in a fast effort. This is **not EMG** (it is a mechanical-deformation proxy, Tier 0 by this document's rulebook) but it is direct evidence that the pelvic floor responds mechanically, and in a modifiable/pre-activatable way, to sudden ballistic loading generally — the closest available non-EMG analogue to what a golf downswing would demand. **Claim licensed:** pelvic floor almost certainly participates mechanically in a ballistic golf swing's IAP bracing (Tier 0 mechanical necessity, per F-046's own logic). **Claim not licensed:** any %MVC, timing offset in milliseconds, or displacement magnitude specific to golf.

No source located anywhere (this session, or the prior 67-source pass) reports diaphragm, TrA, or pelvic floor EMG/pressure during an actual axial-rotation-dominant ballistic task (i.e. Tier 1 or 2 for this specific muscle group does not exist in the literature at all) — every source found is either quasi-static bracing/lifting (sagittal-plane loaded) or isolated twisting at low speed (Cresswell 1992). This is consistent with F-049's invasiveness argument: the electrode/catheter methods needed cannot survive a fast dynamic task, so the literature itself structurally cannot produce Tier 1/2 data for this group, golf or non-golf.

### 3.2 Intrinsic foot muscles, tibialis posterior, flexor hallucis longus

- **Source: Reber, Perry & Pink 1993, PMID 8291630, *Am J Sports Med* — fine-wire EMG, 15 runners, 3 paces.** Tibialis posterior (with gastrocnemius, soleus) showed **peak activity in midstance**, working **eccentrically** to control ankle dorsiflexion as the body's centre of gravity passes over the foot — i.e. its primary functional role is *braking/control of an already-loaded, already-planted foot*, not force generation. Tibialis anterior fired above fatigue threshold for **85% of stance**. Peroneus brevis activity **increased significantly with running pace**. Tier: 2 (shared mechanical demand — controlled loading of a planted foot under body weight — but running gait, not a rotational/pivoting task, and no golf-specific pace equivalent exists). **Claim licensed:** tibialis posterior is very likely working eccentrically (braking/control), not concentrically (propulsive), whenever the golf lead foot is loaded and stable — directly useful for constraining contraction-mode assignment (T-046). Peroneus brevis activity plausibly scales with movement speed/force generally. **Not licensed:** any %MVC value (none reported even in the source), or direct transfer of the *midstance* timing label to a specific golf swing phase.

- **Source: Kelly, Kuitunen, Racinais & Cresswell 2012, PMID 21864955, *Clin Biomech* — surface/intramuscular hybrid, 10 healthy adults, not open access (full-text access blocked; effect sizes only from abstract).** Abductor hallucis, flexor digitorum brevis and quadratus plantae all activated **significantly more in single-leg than double-leg stance** (effect sizes 0.40–0.83) and moved together in tight coordination (inter-muscle correlation ≥0.85), amplitude scaling with medial centre-of-pressure deviation. Tier: 2 (single-leg/asymmetric-loading stance is mechanically shared with golf's weight-transfer through the swing, though static not dynamic). **Claim licensed:** intrinsic foot muscles very likely activate more heavily during golf's single-leg-dominant loading phases (top of backswing, follow-through) than during the more double-leg-loaded address/impact window, and they likely co-recruit as a functional unit rather than independently — directly useful for T-046 constraint propagation. **Not licensed:** any %MVC number (none in the accessible abstract) or dynamic/ballistic-task equivalence (source task is static standing, golf's foot loading is dynamic and much faster).

- **Flexor hallucis longus specifically:** no dedicated non-golf EMG source with numeric values was found in this session despite three targeted search attempts; it appears only as a name alongside tibialis posterior in review/anatomy contexts. This narrows, but does not close, F-044's existing gap finding.

### 3.3 Deep hip rotators (piriformis, obturators, quadratus femoris), psoas, iliacus

- **Source: Andersson, Nilsson & Thorstensson 1997, PMID 9401589, *Acta Physiol Scand* — fine-wire EMG, 11 subjects, walking and running 1.0–6.0 m/s.** Psoas and iliacus (plus sartorius, rectus femoris, tensor fasciae latae) show a **fixed activation-pattern structure** (same number of bursts) regardless of speed or gait mode, but **burst amplitude increases markedly above 2.0 m/s**, with a further step-change between walking and running. Tier: 2/3 (hip flexion during gait shares the "swing a limb from a rotating pelvis" demand with golf's trail-hip mechanics, but sagittal-dominant, not axial-rotation-dominant). **Claim licensed:** psoas/iliacus activation amplitude is very likely velocity-scaled in a golf swing too (mechanical necessity — faster clubhead speed almost certainly means higher psoas/iliacus drive, consistent with force-velocity principles cited in F-037), and the underlying neural activation *pattern* (number/order of bursts) is probably conserved rather than reorganised at swing speed. **Not licensed:** any %MVC number (not reported) or golf-specific timing.
- **Source: PMID 39518756, *J Clin Med* 2024 — hip flexor rehabilitation-exercise EMG.** Iliopsoas exceeded **60% MVIC** during active straight-leg raise near 60° hip flexion, and reached **40–60% MVIC** in mid-range (~45°) positions. Tier: 3 (isolated rehabilitation exercise, no rotational or dynamic-sport element). **Claim licensed:** psoas/iliopsoas is mechanically capable of reaching moderate-to-high %MVC in hip-flexed positions comparable to golf's flexed trail hip — supports plausibility of meaningful iliopsoas involvement, nothing more. **Not licensed:** any specific golf-swing magnitude.
- **Piriformis, obturator internus/externus, quadratus femoris:** **zero EMG studies of any kind located for any dynamic task** (walking, running, sport, or otherwise) across three independently targeted search attempts. What little piriformis EMG literature exists in the medical literature is diagnostic needle EMG for denervation/sciatic entrapment (e.g. Kurková et al. 2025, PMID 40893505, H-reflex latency only), not activity-during-movement data. **This confirms F-049's logic extends beyond the muscles it already names**: the deep hip rotators are functionally in the same "essentially unmeasured, and not just by golf studies" category as the diaphragm/pelvic floor, though for a different reason (small, deep, technically hard to isolate even with fine-wire, rather than requiring catheterisation) — see §5.

### 3.4 Deep spinal muscles (multifidus, rotatores, semispinalis, quadratus lumborum)

- **Source: PMID 33344022, PMCID PMC7727425, *Sports Health* 2020, open access — the richest new numeric dataset for this group.** 19 healthy adults, surface/fine-wire EMG of lumbar multifidus (LM), internal oblique (IO), external oblique (EO), rectus femoris, semitendinosus during **rhythmical standing trunk-twisting** at two speeds (150/90 bpm) and three lower-body positions (straight leg / athletic / dynamic knee extension), reported as true %MVC, plus comparison to static core exercises. **Multifidus values (150 bpm, dominant side): straight-leg 39%, athletic 41%, dynamic-knee-extension 51% MVC** — i.e. multifidus activity **rose with lower-body loading demand even though the trunk-twisting motion itself was constant**, and rose further with cadence (35–44% at 90bpm vs 39–51% at 150bpm). For comparison, static exercises gave: single-leg bridge LM 49%, bird-dog LM 46%, narrow half-kneeling LM 26% — i.e. **dynamic rhythmic twisting at speed produces multifidus activation of the same order of magnitude as classic static core-stability exercises**, not less. Internal oblique showed the opposite coupling — *higher* in the least-braced (straight-leg) position (56–62%) than in more braced positions (24–45%), interpreted by the source authors as lower-body extensor contraction *attenuating* IO demand. Tier: 2 (a genuine trunk-rotation task, at speed, but standing/twisting rather than golf's full kinematic-chain ballistic swing, and no external striking implement). **Claim licensed:** multifidus is very likely substantially active (30–50%+ MVC range) throughout a rotational task at golf-relevant cadences, and its activation is coupled to lower-body loading state, not just trunk angle — directly useful for T-046's constraint propagation between lower-limb loading phase and deep spinal engagement. **Not licensed:** the exact percentages as golf values, or the IO-attenuation-by-leg-position finding as a golf-specific effect without further check against golf's own weight-shift pattern.
- **Rotatores, semispinalis:** no EMG source of any kind (golf or non-golf) located in this session. **Quadratus lumborum:** two studies found that measured it during rotationally-relevant tasks (unilateral countermovement jump, PMID 36612542; asymmetric manual material handling, PMID 34796167) but **neither reports a numeric %MVC value in the accessible abstract** — full text not retrieved this session (flagged for a future access pass, same pattern as the trunk-rotator moment-arm paywall gap already logged in F-037).

### 3.5 Neck muscles (head stabilisation during fast whole-body rotation)

This is the **weakest-evidenced group found in this entire document**, non-golf or golf. Despite four independently targeted search strategies (direct neck-EMG-during-rotation, head-stabilisation-during-locomotion, golf-specific head/neck, and whiplash/rotational-perturbation literature), no source gives a %MVC or amplitude value for sternocleidomastoid, splenius capitis, or cervical erector spinae during any voluntary fast rotational sporting task.
- **Source: Sadeghi-Demneh & Melvin 2014, PMID 25008409, *J Neurophysiol*, open access — vestibulocollic reflex study.** Reports that splenius capitis reflex-evoked EMG magnitude **decreased by ~25%** and sternocleidomastoid by **~30–45%** when comparing cocontraction to focal contraction conditions with the head mechanically fixed — i.e. neck-muscle recruitment strategy for head stabilisation is context-dependent and reflexive, but this is a passive-perturbation paradigm, not a voluntary-rotation task, and reports relative not absolute values. Tier: 3 at best (generic function only: confirms these muscles participate in reflexive head-stabilisation control, nothing about magnitude in a sporting context).
- **Source: PMID 39866562, *PeerJ* 2025 — Judo osoto-gari head/trunk kinematics (not EMG).** Found forward head-tilt rotation contributes to leg-drive velocity (R²=0.53 predictive model), evidence that head/neck positioning is *functionally coupled* to whole-body rotational power output in at least one other rotational combat sport — kinematic, not muscle-activation, evidence, but directly supports the mechanical-necessity argument (Tier 3 anatomical-obligation route) that neck muscles must be doing *something* functionally significant during fast axial rotation, without licensing any numeric claim.
**Consequence:** neck muscles can be assigned presence/plausibility only (Tier 3, weakest form — existence argument, not even a generic-function %MVC), corroborating and extending this project's own F-057 finding that head/neck data is a genuine literature gap in golf kinematics specifically — it is now confirmed to be a broader gap in the sports-science and motor-control literature generally, not a golf-specific omission.

## 4. Rotational-sport EMG (closest analogue tier — Tier 1)

Six candidate sports were searched (baseball pitching, tennis serve, discus, hammer, javelin, cricket bowling, ice hockey slap shot, batting). Coverage is uneven: trunk/hip EMG exists in usable numeric form for **baseball pitching, tennis serve, and ice hockey shooting**; **discus/hammer/javelin/cricket bowling/batting trunk-hip EMG could not be located** despite repeated targeted search (discus: only shoulder/arm EMG found, Dinu et al. 2019, PMID 31440410, no trunk/hip/leg data; javelin, hammer, cricket bowling, cricket/baseball batting: zero trunk/hip/leg EMG studies found in any search attempted). This is itself a finding: the closest mechanical analogues to golf by throw/strike type are, in practice, data-poor outside baseball pitching/tennis/ice hockey.

### 4.1 Tennis serve — richest dataset found (Source: Chow et al. 2009, PMID 19825184, PMCID PMC2770553, *Sports Med Arthrosc Rehabil Ther Technol*, open access, CC BY)
19 male players (11 advanced/AV, 8 advanced-intermediate/AI); bilateral surface EMG of rectus abdominis, external oblique, internal oblique, erector spinae during flat, topspin and slice serves, normalised to each trial's own peak EMG (**not MVC** — values can and do exceed 100, so these are within-trial relative, not absolute %MVC; flagged per §1.3's normalisation-method caveat). All six muscles peaked in the **acceleration phase** in most flat/topspin conditions, consistent with this project's own golf trunk-rotator finding (trail EO/IO peak in acceleration, `02-trunk-and-core-activation.md`). Selected AV flat-serve values (ascending / descending / acceleration / follow-through, % of trial peak): left rectus abdominis 25/62/**92**/28; left external oblique 44/121/110/5; **left internal oblique 51/183/118/59** (highest overall reading in the dataset); right internal oblique 30/69/116/88; left/right erector spinae peak later, in follow-through for the right side (72, vs 42 in acceleration) — consistent with erector spinae's stabilising/deceleration role rather than a driving role. **Tier 1 claim supported:** relative activation *ordering* across trunk muscles and phase-timing pattern (obliques dominate acceleration, erector spinae holds into follow-through). **Not supported:** absolute %MVC magnitude (wrong normalisation reference) or the exact numbers as golf values.

### 4.2 Baseball pitching (Source: Watkins et al. 1989, PMID 2524110, *Spine*, not open access — abstract only)
15 professional pitchers, surface EMG of bilateral abdominal oblique, rectus abdominis, lumbar paraspinous and gluteus maximus across five pitching phases, referenced against isometric maximum. Finding: **abdominal oblique, lumbar paraspinous and rectus abdominis contralateral to the throwing arm, plus ipsilateral gluteus maximus, all increased 75–100% during active pitching** relative to baseline/isometric reference. **Tier 1 claim supported:** existence and rough magnitude-band of contralateral trunk-rotator recruitment plus ipsilateral glute drive in a fast unilateral rotational/throwing task — directly analogous in structure to golf's trail-side drive mechanics, independently reinforcing this project's own oblique/glute golf findings from an entirely separate sport, lab and era. **Not supported:** phase-by-phase numeric breakdown (not extractable from the abstract; full text paywalled, T-040-style licence/access flag for a future session).

### 4.3 Ice hockey slap shot / wrist shot (Source: PMID 40142898, PMCID PMC11942957, *J Clin Med* 2025, open access — one of the three most valuable datasets found, see summary)
21 male ice hockey players, surface EMG (%MVIC) of rectus abdominis, external oblique, internal oblique, erector spinae across six shot/surface conditions (ground vs ice, standing vs skating, slap vs wrist). On-ice skating slap shot (the closest mechanical analogue to a full golf swing — a rotational strike generated from a moving, unstable base): **rectus abdominis 28.6%, external oblique 42.9%, internal oblique 43.7%, erector spinae 73.6%** MVIC. Ice conditions consistently exceeded ground conditions for every muscle (e.g. erector spinae 73.6% ice-skating vs 59.4% ground, both slap shot), and the authors explicitly link the mechanism to golf: *"to effectively execute trunk rotation in sports like tennis strokes or golf swings, the strength of the trunk muscles is also necessary to generate speed and force during rotation."* **Tier 1 claim supported:** true %MVIC magnitude band for trunk rotators in a fast, ballistic, base-unstable rotational strike — the single closest structural analogue to golf's mechanical demand found in this search (unstable/low-friction base, full-body rotational transfer into a single ballistic strike). **Not supported:** exact numeric transplantation to golf (different implement, one-handed vs two-handed grip effects on trunk loading, different base — ice vs turf — and no golf-specific validation exists).

### 4.4 Cross-sport corroboration
The trunk-rotator recruitment **pattern** — obliques peak during the acceleration/drive phase, erector spinae peaks later (deceleration/follow-through), contralateral-side dominance for the primary rotator — replicates independently across tennis serve (§4.1), baseball pitching (§4.2), ice hockey shooting (§4.3), and this project's own golf-specific EMG (`02-trunk-and-core-activation.md`, Marta 2013 trail EO). Four independent sports, four independent labs, one consistent qualitative pattern. Per the DC framework (§1.1) this is exactly the kind of convergent, mechanism-matched evidence that legitimately raises confidence in the *timing/ordering* claim without ever licensing the *magnitude* claim — magnitude varies 2–3× across the four sports for directly comparable muscles even where methodology is similar (e.g. tennis internal oblique acceleration-phase values of 100–138 vs ice hockey internal oblique of 36–44%MVIC — different normalisation references, contraction dynamics and implement loading, exactly as §1.3 predicts).

**No study located directly compares golf to any other rotational sport's EMG in the same subjects/protocol** — confirmed by a dedicated search; this would be the single most valuable possible source and does not exist. This is a genuine literature gap, not a search failure (cross-checked against this project's F-058 finding that even golf's own kinematics literature suffers from low independent-lab count).

## 5. Limits — what remains unfillable

Even with non-golf transfer fully exploited, several categories remain unfillable to any numeric (%MVC/timing) standard, for two structurally distinct reasons that must be labelled differently in the schema (T-042):

**(A) Unfillable because the measurement method itself cannot survive the target task, in golf or anywhere else (confirms and extends F-049).** Diaphragm, pelvic floor and TrA during a fast axial-rotation ballistic task; piriformis/obturator internus/obturator externus/quadratus femoris during any dynamic sporting task; rotatores and semispinalis at any speed. For these, **no amount of non-golf searching will find Tier 1 or Tier 2 EMG data, because it structurally does not exist** — the literature itself is capped by the same electrode-invasiveness ceiling that blocks golf-specific measurement (fine-wire/catheter methods degrade or become unsafe at speed, and are rarely attempted even in slow non-golf tasks for the deepest muscles). This is now confirmed as a literature-wide ceiling, not a golf-research-community gap: three of four target muscle groups in this document's brief (deep hip rotators, rotatores/semispinalis, and dynamic-task diaphragm/pelvic floor/TrA) returned zero dynamic-task EMG sources of any kind, golf or non-golf, across multiple independent search strategies.

**(B) Unfillable to numeric precision because the closest available transfer sources fail the Dynamic Correspondence test on multiple criteria simultaneously, not just one.** Neck muscles: the vestibulocollic-reflex data (§3.5) fails on regime of muscular work (reflexive/passive-perturbation vs voluntary athletic effort), rate of force production, and task context; the Judo kinematic data fails because it is not EMG at all. Both individually would license only the weakest tier (existence/plausibility), and combining two weak-tier sources does not upgrade the tier — DC criteria must be matched by a single coherent source or a converging set of *sport-relevant* sources, not stacked from unrelated fields.

**What non-golf transfer DOES legitimately fill**, per the tiers established in §2 and evidenced in §3–4: presence/absence of a muscle's involvement (all groups), coarse magnitude bands and cross-muscle ranking within a task-type family (multifidus, obliques, tibialis posterior, iliopsoas, pelvic floor mechanical response), and phase-relative timing/ordering patterns that replicate across ≥3 independent rotational-sport sources (obliques-then-erector-spinae drive-then-stabilise sequencing, §4.4). It does not, and per the DC/EMG-specificity literature in §1 cannot, fill exact %MVC magnitudes, exact millisecond timing, or contraction-mode assignment without a task-matched source — and for the muscles named in (A) above, no task-matched source of any kind currently exists in the published literature.
