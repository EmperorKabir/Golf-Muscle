What I'd build, and why

Study 02 as the base, with the depth handling from 03 and the reveal rule from 09. A dark field is the only ground on which "transparent" actually reads as transparent — on white, an unused muscle and empty space look identical, which defeats the contrast you asked for. Depth fading is what stops a see-through body from turning into a tangle of lines once it rotates. And letting quiet muscles sink to a bare outline means the eye goes straight to what is working, without ever losing the full anatomical map you wanted kept for contrast.

Two things I would not carry forward: fibre hatching (05) costs a great deal of rendering work to communicate something the outline shape already implies, and stepped bands (08) impose false precision on data that is genuinely smooth.

  1. Tell me which study, or which mix, to take forward.
  2. The muscle-zone list gets finalised from the research now running, then the zone borders are rebuilt to match it exactly.
  3. The chosen treatment is written up as the rendering specification, and the 3D build starts against it.

On accuracy: the colours here are placeholders, set from general knowledge of the swing so the panels have something plausible to show. They are not the final numbers. The real values come from the cross-referenced research now in progress, and every muscle's value will carry its source. Treat these panels as a judgement of look only. The pose is also a simplification — a symmetric address-style stance, drawn front-on. The real model will be asymmetric and fully three-dimensional; back-of-body muscles (glutes, hamstrings, lats from behind, the whole posterior chain) do not appear here at all and are a large part of the swing.
